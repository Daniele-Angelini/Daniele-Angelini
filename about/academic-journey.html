Academic journey

From physics to mathematical finance

My academic path developed through a gradual shift from theoretical physics toward probability, stochastic processes and mathematical finance. The common thread has remained the same: using mathematical structure to understand complex and irregular phenomena.

01

Physics as a starting point

My first academic training was in physics. It provided the language that still shapes the way I approach mathematical problems: models, limiting arguments, approximation and the search for structures that remain visible beneath apparently complicated behaviour.

02

Theoretical physics and mathematical methods

Theoretical physics strengthened my interest in the mathematical side of modelling. Probability, analysis and the study of complex systems gradually became more central, creating a natural bridge toward stochastic modelling.

03

Moving toward mathematical finance

Mathematical finance offered a setting in which stochastic processes, statistical inference and real-world data interact continuously. My research developed around fractional and multifractional processes, roughness, dependence and the local regularity of volatility.

04

Research and teaching today

My current work combines theoretical questions, statistical methodology and empirical applications. Teaching is a complementary part of this activity: explaining mathematical ideas clearly often reveals connections that are less visible when working only at research level.
