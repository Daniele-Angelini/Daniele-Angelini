Welcome and learning agreement

The course is mathematically demanding, but no topic will be presented as a sequence of formulas to be memorised. Each subject will be developed through a stable sequence: intuition, definition, example, counterexample, theorem and exercise.

The complete programme remains unchanged. The large slide and exercise collections are reference libraries: the page will always distinguish core material, recommended practice and optional extensions, so that the expected workload is visible and manageable.

Objectives & Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

  • use the basic language of sets, real numbers, intervals and inequalities correctly;
  • work with vectors and matrices, analyse linear dependence and rank, and solve systems of linear equations;
  • analyse real-valued functions through their domains, graphs, monotonicity, invertibility and composition;
  • compute and interpret limits, identify discontinuities and apply the main results for continuous functions;
  • compute derivatives and use them to study monotonicity, extrema, convexity, concavity, inflection points and asymptotic behaviour;
  • perform a complete qualitative analysis of a function and construct its graph from analytical information;
  • work with elementary functions of two variables, compute partial derivatives and determine unconstrained maxima and minima;
  • compute definite and indefinite integrals and apply the main integration techniques;
  • connect algebraic calculations with their geometric and graphical interpretations;
  • present mathematical arguments and solutions clearly, select appropriate methods and verify the hypotheses required by the results used.

Six-module course roadmap

The official programme is organised into six modules. A two-lesson prerequisite bridge precedes the modules, and the final lesson is devoted to consolidation and a complete mock examination. Topics from the completion package are integrated into their natural module rather than counted as an additional module.

Course structure Mathematical Foundations · 6 modules
Module 0 Mathematical foundations 1 lecture

16 September 2026

Topics: sets and set notation, subsets, unions and intersections, real numbers, intervals, inequalities, absolute value and distance on the real line.

Purpose: establish the mathematical language used throughout the course and provide a rapid diagnostic review of essential prerequisites.

Module 1 Linear algebra 8 lessons

...

Core topics: vectors, operations with vectors, scalar multiplication, linear combinations, convex linear combinations, vector spaces and subspaces, linear dependence and independence, rank of a set of vectors, matrices, matrix operations and matrix multiplication, determinants, Sarrus' rule, minors, rank of a matrix, systems of linear equations, Rouché–Capelli theorem, Cramer's theorem, homogeneous and parametric systems.

Geometric focus: vectors as displacements, vector addition and subtraction, span, linear dependence, determinant as signed area/volume, rank as dimensional information, and systems as intersections of geometric objects.

Practice focus: frequent graphical exercises, matrix calculations and progressively more complex linear systems.

Module 2 Functions, limits and continuity 8 lectures

...

Core topics: real functions of a real variable, domain and range, elementary and quasi-elementary functions, graphs, transformations, monotonicity, invertibility, inverse functions, composite functions, limits, one-sided limits, uniqueness and comparison theorems, operations with limits, continuous functions, discontinuities and the main theorems on continuous functions.

Visual focus: graphs are used systematically to interpret domain, range, monotonicity, inverse functions, composition, limiting behaviour and discontinuities.

Practice focus: reading graphs, reconstructing functions from qualitative information, calculating domains and limits, and connecting analytical results with graphical behaviour.

Module 3 Differential calculus 10 lectures

...

Core topics: definition of derivative, geometric interpretation, differentiation rules, derivatives of powers, exponentials and logarithms, local increase and decrease, Rolle's theorem, Lagrange's and Cauchy's mean value theorems, monotonicity, extrema, indeterminate forms, de l'Hôpital's theorem, differential, chain rule, higher-order derivatives, convexity, concavity, inflection points and Taylor's formula with Lagrange remainder.

Module 4 Function analysis 2 lectures

...

Core topics: asymptotes and complete qualitative study of a real function, domain, intercepts, sign, symmetry, limits, asymptotes, first derivative, monotonicity, extrema, second derivative, convexity, concavity and inflection points are combined into a single coherent workflow.

Module 5 Multivariable functions 2 lectures

...

Core topics: functions of two variables, domains in the plane, graphical representation, level curves, partial derivatives, critical points and unconstrained maxima and minima.

Module 6 Integral calculus 5 lectures

...

Core topics: definition of integral, geometric interpretation, properties of the definite integral, mean value theorem, integral function, fundamental theorem of calculus, antiderivatives, definite and indefinite integrals, integration by decomposition, transformation, substitution and integration by parts.

Workload and exercise policy

The complete slide decks and exercise collections are intentionally extensive. They provide coverage, alternative explanations and long-term exam practice; they are not all assigned at once.

Core Expected weekly work

Definitions highlighted in class and four representative exercises per lesson.

Recommended Consolidation

Four additional exercises selected to vary method and difficulty.

Optional Advanced practice

Two challenge problems, longer proofs and extension slides for deeper study.

The exercise banks are libraries

The 100-plus exercise packs are not weekly compulsory lists. A dated exercise sheet will identify the small subset expected for the next lesson.

Assessment and examination transparency

The final written examination is the confirmed assessment component. A single assessment sheet will collect all rules before the first lesson; no examination requirement will be communicated only orally.

Format Written examination
Question types Short theory, method selection and multi-step exercises
Programming No programming-language syntax is assessed
Detailed specification Published by 16 September 2026

The assessment sheet will state explicitly:

  • duration and total score;
  • number and type of questions;
  • score assigned to every part;
  • minimum requirements and passing rule;
  • permitted materials and calculator policy;
  • role and rules of any partial assessment;
  • criteria for mathematical correctness, reasoning and presentation.

Progress test and mock examination

A formative progress test will be provided after Module 3. The final lesson is reserved for a complete mock written examination and public correction. Any graded partial assessment will be announced, with complete rules, before teaching begins.
